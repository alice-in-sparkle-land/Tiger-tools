Fisher Backtest Builder

Declare the columns and rules. Get one .py that backtests them on your data.

1. Columns in df

df is your transaction-level Spark DataFrame, one row per transaction. Don't aggregate it first.

Count by

Customers: a person counts once, however many times they pay (the safer test). Transactions: every payment counts, which also catches a few customers paying many times. Tick both to compare them in the backtest.

2. Metrics

Flag share: any SQL condition on a transaction. Amount flag: amount above or below a value. Volume: the seller's share of all transactions (self test) or of its peer group (peer test).

3. Comparisons

Days counted back from each anchor day; day 1 is the anchor itself. Every day in the data is an anchor (rolling backtest).

Anchor range (optional)

4. Peer check

Peers = the other sellers with the same values in these columns, in the same recent window. The seller itself is always left out. Counting by customers, its own customers are left out of the peer row too, so the two rows never share a person. When the group has fewer other sellers than the minimum, the fallback group (coarser, e.g. seller_group alone) is used instead; detail.peer_level says which.

5. Labels (optional)

Transaction level. Leave blank to skip precision, recall and default lift.

A seller is suspicious from the date of its first suspicious transaction (or the flagged-on date). Lift after alert: default rate of matured loans in the N days after each alert, against sellers the rule never alerted. Lift in window (looks back): loans inside the alert's own recent window.

6. Trigger rule

A test runs when the seller has at least min recent total in the recent window and at least min flagged flagged (for volume: its own count). It fires when q (Benjamini–Hochberg over all sellers that day) and the odds ratio both pass. One-sided: only increases fire. Per rule keeps a 5% false-discovery rate for each rule on its own (right for picking one rule); every rule that day keeps it across all the rules together (right when several rules go live at once).
